What is Dogecoin?

Qu'est-ce que Dogecoin ?

¿Qué es Dogecoin?

これは何？

Dogecoin started as a joke. In December 2013, software engineers Billy Markus and Jackson Palmer created it in a weekend as a satirical cryptocurrency — a lighthearted jab at the speculative frenzy surrounding Bitcoin. They slapped the face of the "Doge" Shiba Inu meme on it, gave it a goofy name, and expected it to disappear within months. Instead, it found a community of people who loved its unpretentious, generous spirit, and that community built something real.

The early Dogecoin community became famous for its charitable impulses. In 2014, they raised $50,000 in DOGE to sponsor NASCAR driver Josh Wise, and $25,000 to send the Jamaican bobsled team to the Winter Olympics. These weren't marketing campaigns — they were spontaneous acts of collective generosity. Dogecoin became the "friendly internet money" because that's how its community genuinely behaved.

Fast-forward to the 2020s and Dogecoin's trajectory became inseparable from Elon Musk. His repeated endorsements — calling DOGE the "people's crypto," posting memes, and appearing on Saturday Night Live — drove price surges that turned early holders into millionaires and brought millions of new users to cryptocurrency for the first time. By May 2021, DOGE had reached $0.74, a 12,000% gain from the start of the year. The joke had become a $90 billion asset at its peak. Today, with multiple spot ETFs now approved in the US, Dogecoin has crossed from meme territory into a recognized institutional asset class.

How does it work?

Comment ça fonctionne ?

¿Cómo funciona?

どう機能する？

Dogecoin is a fork of Litecoin, which is itself a fork of Bitcoin — so its technical foundations are well-understood and battle-tested. It uses the Scrypt proof-of-work algorithm, which was originally designed to be more resistant to specialized mining hardware (ASICs) than Bitcoin's SHA-256. Blocks are produced every minute (compared to Bitcoin's 10 minutes), meaning transactions confirm faster and fees are extremely low — typically fractions of a cent.

One of Dogecoin's most distinctive technical features is its monetary policy: there is no maximum supply cap. Approximately 5 billion new DOGE are added to circulation every year through mining rewards (~10,000 DOGE per block). This is intentional — it was designed to discourage hoarding and encourage spending. However, because the yearly issuance is a fixed number (not a fixed percentage of total supply), the inflation rate decreases over time as the total supply grows. At current supply levels, annual inflation is around 3%.

Another key technical feature is merged mining with Litecoin. Since 2014, Dogecoin has allowed miners to simultaneously mine both DOGE and LTC without extra computational work. This dramatically increased the hash rate securing the Dogecoin network, making it far more resistant to 51% attacks than it would be as a standalone network. It means Dogecoin's security is effectively backed by the same mining power that protects Litecoin — a significant technical improvement that often goes unappreciated.

Dogecoin is used for peer-to-peer tips and microtransactions on social media (Reddit, Twitter/X), for purchasing merchandise from Tesla, electronics from Newegg, movie tickets from AMC Theatres, NBA tickets from the Dallas Mavericks, airline tickets from airBaltic, and travel bookings from Travala. It is also used for charitable donations, as a gateway currency for crypto newcomers, and increasingly as a speculative investment vehicle via ETFs on traditional brokerage platforms.

Il est également utilisé pour des dons caritatifs, comme monnaie d'entrée pour les nouveaux venus en crypto, et de plus en plus comme véhicule d'investissement spéculatif via des ETF sur les plateformes de courtage traditionnelles.
