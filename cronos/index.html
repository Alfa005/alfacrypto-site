What is Cronos?

Qu'est-ce que Cronos ?

¿Qué es Cronos?

これは何？

Cronos is the native blockchain of Crypto.com, one of the world's largest cryptocurrency exchanges with over 150 million registered users. But to describe CRO simply as an "exchange token" is to miss the broader picture. CRO started in 2016 as Monaco — a Visa card project promising cryptocurrency cashback rewards. After rebranding to Crypto.com and acquiring the iconic crypto.com domain for $12 million in 2018, the team launched CRO as the central token of a rapidly expanding financial ecosystem that now includes a major exchange, DeFi services, NFT marketplace, and its own blockchain layer.

The Cronos chain itself launched in 2021 as an EVM-compatible Layer 1 blockchain built on the Cosmos SDK. This combination is deliberate and powerful: EVM compatibility means any Ethereum smart contract or dApp can be deployed on Cronos with minimal code changes, instantly giving developers access to the entire Ethereum tooling ecosystem. The Cosmos SDK integration enables the Cronos chain to communicate natively with other Cosmos-based blockchains via the Inter-Blockchain Communication (IBC) protocol — effectively plugging Cronos into a growing network of interoperable chains.

What gives CRO its core value proposition is its role in the Crypto.com ecosystem. Users stake CRO tokens to unlock higher tiers of the Crypto.com Visa card — the more CRO you stake, the more benefits you receive, including up to 5% cashback in CRO, access to airport lounges, and streaming service rebates. This creates a sustained demand mechanism: users need CRO to access premium features, and earning cashback in CRO creates new holders. The system has been adopted by millions of users in over 90 countries.

How does it work?

Comment ça fonctionne ?

¿Cómo funciona?

どう機能する？

Cronos uses a Proof of Authority (PoA) consensus mechanism built on Tendermint (also known as CometBFT). Unlike Proof of Work or even standard Proof of Stake, PoA relies on a curated set of vetted, known validators — rather than anonymous miners or stakers — to produce blocks and validate transactions. This results in very high transaction throughput (over 10,000 TPS), sub-second block finality (~0.5 second blocks), and near-zero transaction fees. The tradeoff is centralization: the validator set is small and permission-based, meaning the chain's security depends on the integrity of approved validators like Ubisoft, Chainnode, and others selected by the Cronos team.

The architecture layers multiple chains. The base Cronos EVM chain handles most DeFi and dApp activity. The Cronos POS chain (formerly Crypto.org Chain) handles the CRO staking, governance, and validator operations. Additionally, a Cronos zkEVM (zero-knowledge EVM) is in development to provide Ethereum Layer 2-style scaling with cryptographic validity proofs — a sign that the team is building for long-term scalability rather than relying solely on PoA centralization.

CRO token economics are notable for their complexity. The maximum supply is 100 billion CRO, with roughly 42 billion in current circulation. A significant portion is held by Crypto.com for ecosystem development, rewards programs, and exchange operations. In March 2025, Cronos controversially reissued 70 billion previously burned CRO tokens back into a reserve fund — a decision that sparked community debate about supply management. This event underscored the degree to which CRO's economics are controlled by Crypto.com, which is both a strength (coordinated ecosystem investment) and a risk (centralized supply decisions).
