What is IOTA?

Qu'est-ce que IOTA ?

¿Qué es IOTA?

これは何？

The Internet of Things (IoT) is the idea that everyday objects — your refrigerator, your car, your electricity meter — are connected to the internet and can share data and make small transactions automatically. The problem is that traditional blockchains are terrible at this: Bitcoin and Ethereum charge fees that are worth more than the transaction value when a sensor wants to send three cents of data payment. IOTA was built to fix this.

Founded in 2015 by David Sønstebø, Dominik Schiener, Sergey Ivancheglo, and Serguei Popov, IOTA originally used a completely different data structure than a blockchain. Instead of a linear chain of blocks, it used something called the Tangle — a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) where every new transaction validates two previous ones. Think of it like a web instead of a chain. This allowed feeless microtransactions: a temperature sensor could pay one-tenth of a cent to submit a data reading without the fee eating the entire value.

In 2025, IOTA underwent the most significant upgrade in its history: the "Rebased" mainnet launch. This replaced the Tangle architecture with a more conventional but dramatically faster system — 50,000+ TPS with MoveVM smart contracts (the same framework used by Aptos and Sui), delegated Proof-of-Stake consensus, and full decentralization. The upgrade also removed the "Coordinator" — the centralized safety node that had been a long-standing criticism of the network — which had already been removed in 2023.

By March 2026, IOTA has a market cap of approximately $255 million and a circulating supply of 4.35 billion tokens. Its real-world deployment portfolio is impressive: supply chain tracking in the EU, trade finance across African markets backed by the World Economic Forum and Tony Blair Institute, and digital identity solutions used in enterprise settings.

How does it work?

Comment ça fonctionne ?

¿Cómo funciona?

どう機能する？

After the Rebased upgrade, IOTA uses a Delegated Proof-of-Stake (dPoS) consensus with Mysticeti and Starfish BFT protocols. In dPoS, token holders delegate their stake to validators they trust, and those validators take turns proposing and confirming blocks. Mysticeti is a low-latency consensus protocol originally developed for the Sui blockchain — it allows multiple validators to propose blocks simultaneously rather than sequentially, dramatically increasing throughput. The result is sub-second transaction finality at 50,000+ TPS.

Smart contracts on IOTA now use the Move programming language via MoveVM. Move was designed by Facebook's blockchain team with safety as the primary goal — it uses an "ownership model" that prevents common smart contract bugs like reentrancy attacks that have caused hundreds of millions in DeFi hacks. This makes IOTA's smart contract environment significantly safer than Ethereum's Solidity at the architectural level.

The Layer-2 ecosystem is growing rapidly. IOTA EVM runs an Ethereum-compatible environment on top of the base layer, letting Solidity developers deploy their existing contracts without rewriting them. Cross-chain connectivity comes from LayerZero and Stargate integrations, connecting IOTA to 30+ chains. The feeless base layer remains a key differentiator for IoT use cases: a supply chain sensor submitting 1,000 readings per day incurs zero transaction costs, making data integrity tracking economically viable at scale.
